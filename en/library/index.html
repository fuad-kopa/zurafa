Zurafa — Timur Chess
RUUZTRZHHI

The library

What is known about Timur’s great chess: the game, the people, the manuscripts and the places. Every article lists its sources; disputed points are marked as disputed.

  • Great chessShatranj kabir, “great chess”: the game of Timur’s court on an 11 × 10 board with two citadels.
  • The names of the pieces, and why “Zurafa”Shah, ferz, vazir, zurafa, tali’a, faras, rukh, pil, jamal, dabbaba: what the old names mean.
  • The citadelsTwo projecting squares that change how a game ends: a king that reaches the enemy citadel saves the game.
  • The pawn of pawns, the prince and the adventitious kingThe trickiest rules of great chess: a pawn that promotes three times, and kings that can multiply.
  • The family of great chessSmall chess, “complete chess”, citadel chess, and Turkish great chess on 13 × 13.
  • Shatranj, the small chessThe game on an 8 × 8 board that everyone at Timur’s court knew, and against which great chess was measured.
  • Nard, Bozorgmehr’s answerThe game of dice and checkers that Persian tradition calls a sage’s reply to Indian chess.
  • Toguz korgool, nine pebblesThe steppe game of two rows of nine holes: counting, reckoning, and one trap hole, the tuzdyk.
  • Asyk, the knuckleboneA sheep’s ankle bone: humanity’s oldest gaming piece and the favourite game of the steppe.
  • Timur and chessThe ruler at whose court great chess was played, and a son named, by tradition, after a chess move.
  • Ali ash-Shatranji“Ali the Chess Player” from Tabriz: the strongest player at Timur’s court, and possibly the author of the treatise that preserves the rules.
  • Ibn ArabshahA boy from Damascus taken to Samarkand, who grew into Timur’s most hostile biographer and preserved the names of the chessmen.
  • How Europe learnt about great chessHyde, Bland, Forbes, Murray: four scholars who drew the game out of the manuscripts over two centuries.
  • The London manuscript, MS 211The only text from which we know the rules of great chess, and its road through India to London.
  • Nafa’is al-fununA fourteenth-century Persian encyclopedia that describes the great chess board.
  • Samarkand of Timur and Ulugh BegThe capital for which Timur brought craftsmen from every corner of his empire.
  • Afrasiab: among the oldest chessmenSeven ivory pieces from ancient Samarkand, evidence that chess was played here long before Timur.
  • Oblong chessShatranj on a 4 × 16 board where a die decides which piece moves: the earliest known chess played with dice.
  • Complete chess, 10 × 10Shatranj at-tamma: the first known attempt to enlarge the board and add a piece, the dabbaba, a siege engine.
  • Citadel chessShatranj al-husun from al-Amuli’s encyclopaedia: a 10 × 10 board, bishop-like dabbabas and four strongholds at the corners.
  • Round chessShatranj ar-rumiya, “Byzantine chess”: 64 squares in four rings, pawns that never promote, and one more game from the house of Ali ash-Shatranji.
  • Chatrang: chess under the SasaniansHow the game came from India to Iran, who Bozorgmehr was, and why nard is the answer to chess.
  • Mansubat: the problems of the old mastersArranged positions won by a run of checks and sacrifices, and the legend of Dilaram, saved by two rooks.
  • Ganjifa: the cards of the Timurids and MughalsRound cards in eight suits, sent as gifts by Babur and banned by Shah Abbas II.
  • Al-Adli and as-SuliTwo Baghdad masters of the ninth and tenth centuries whose books preserved the rules, openings and problems of shatranj, and oblong chess with the die.
  • Clavijo: an envoy in SamarkandThe Castilian knight who spent three months of 1404 at Timur’s court and left the best European description of his capital.
  • Chess and the lawIs chess lawful for a Muslim? The four schools of law answered differently, and Ali ash-Shatranji was a Shafi’ite.
  • Herat of Shah Rukh and BaysunghurThe second Timurid capital: the city where the Herat school of painting was born and where shatranj players were painted.
  • Shahrisabz: Timur’s birthplaceAncient Kesh, the “Green City”, where Timur was born and built a palace whose gate still stands.
